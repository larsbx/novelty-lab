(q) odd, quadratic norm (N), and polar form (B). No real normalization,

## N2-T03 — square-root-free defect theorem

For (x,yin C^	imes_N), define
[
Delta(x,y)=L_xL_yL_{xy}^{-1}.

Then:

1. (Delta(x,y)in O(C,N));
2. (Delta(x,y)(xy)=xy);
3. (Delta(x,y)=I) if and only if
   ([x,y,z]=x(yz)-(xy)z=0) for every (zin C).

**Proof.** Composition gives
[
N(L_xz)=N(x)N(z),
]
so (L_x) is a similitude with multiplier (N(x)). Since
(N(xy)=N(x)N(y)), the multiplier of
(L_xL_yL_{xy}^{-1}) is one; hence (Delta(x,y)) is an isometry. Also
(L_{xy}^{-1}(xy)=1), so
[
Delta(x,y)(xy)=L_xL_y(1)=xy.
]
Finally, (Delta(x,y)=I) precisely when
(L_xL_y=L_{xy}), which is equivalent, after evaluation at every (z), to
(x(yz)=(xy)z). ∎

This theorem is strictly finite and algebraic. The defect lies in the
orthogonal stabilizer of the nonisotropic vertex (xy). Passing to projective
classes gives a discrete rotational relation without selecting a square root.

## What Burnside does—and does not—supply

## Falsifiable replacement claim

> For a pinned finite octonion model and admissible navigation alphabet, the
> square-root-free defects (Delta(x,y)) define a finite orthogonal action.
> If the observation map is invariant under that action, its quotient-fiber
> occupancies are exactly certified by Burnside fixed-point tables. For longer
> words, rebracketing is captured by defect-labelled edges; nontrivial cycle
> holonomy is a finite obstruction to parenthesization independence.

The first two sentences reduce to explicit group, invariance, and fixed-point
checks. The last sentence is a research target: edge transport and cycle
holonomy must be defined and verified, starting with the five
parenthesizations of a four-letter word.
