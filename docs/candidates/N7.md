# N7 — Finite projective arithmetic

**Status:** candidate

Compare three interfaces on the same exact finite vector classes. Let
\(q>2\) be a prime power, \(V=\mathbb F_q^n\) with \(n\ge2\), and
\(X=[x]\) for \(x\ne0\) modulo nonzero scalar multiplication. Write
\(O_X\) for the representative orbit, and adjoin a distinct arithmetic
zero \(\mathbf0\) with \(O_{\mathbf0}=\{0\}\).

## Set-valued incidence sum

For distinct nonzero classes, return the projective join with its two
endpoints removed. For equal nonzero classes \(X\), return
\(\{\mathbf0,X\}\). Adding \(\mathbf0\) returns the other class as a
singleton. Compare the incidence rule against the projected sums of all
representative pairs. For \(n=3\), recover each projective line by restoring
the two endpoints, and compare joins and meets with exact rank and
intersection computations.

## Multiset-valued incidence sum

For each output class \(Z\), count the ordered pairs in \(O_X\times O_Y\)
whose vector sum equals a fixed \(z\in O_Z\). Simultaneous rescaling
relates the counts at different representatives of \(Z\). The contract
uses natural-number coefficients. For distinct nonzero inputs the
allowed classes have coefficient one. For equal nonzero inputs,
\(\mathbf0\) has coefficient \(q-1\) and \(X\) has coefficient \(q-2\).
Forgetting counts should recover the set-valued sum.

Start from the orbit sums in the additive group algebra for the
associativity proof. Keep fixed-output counts separate from raw counts
of pairs landing anywhere in an output orbit. The latter should fail
the identity and associativity controls. With an explicit identification
of \(V\) with \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\), extension-field multiplication induces
single-valued multiplication on classes. Nonzero multiplication
permutes representatives and preserves the counts. Test the separate
zero-scalar boundary using \(\mathbb F_7\), where the repeated-input sum
collapses to eleven copies of zero under multiset pushforward and
\(\mathbf0\boxplus\mathbf0\) contains one.

## Framed scalar arithmetic

On \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)\), choose three distinct classes labelled
\(0,1,\infty\). In the coordinates of that frame, the sum and product of
\([a:b]\) and \([c:d]\) are \([ad+bc:bd]\) and \([ac:bd]\) whenever
their output pairs are nonzero. Test ordinary field agreement on the
affine chart and covariance when the inputs and frame are transported
together. Represent a zero output pair as undefined. The framed
coordinate zero \([0:1]\) and the adjoined incidence zero
\(\mathbf0\) have different types and roles.

## First evidence

Use \(V=\mathbb F_7^2\) and the multiplication model
\(\mathbb F_7[u]/(u^2-3)\). Exhaust all nine-element triples for the
multiset laws and check all representative rescalings for the framed
operations. Add \(\mathbb F_3^3\) for nontrivial joins and intersections.
The \(\mathbb F_2\) self-sum has only the zero outcome and is a boundary
control outside the stated \(q>2\) contract. Replay should compare
coefficient-by-coefficient results and include the full finite domains.

The prior-art gate should compare the precise incidence, orbit-sum and
framed contracts against quotient hyperfields, orbit convolution and
classical coordinatization. The possible contribution is an exact
shared-fixture comparison with independently replayable evidence.
General reusable projective arithmetic belongs upstream in
finite-math-kernels, with Lean here serving as a supporting oracle.

Sources to start from include [Krasner's quotient construction](https://doi.org/10.1155/S0161171283000265),
[Connes and Consani, Proposition 3.1](https://math.jhu.edu/~kc/papers/JNT2.pdf),
[Wildberger, Section 3](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~norman/papers/FiniteHypergroupsSurvey.pdf),
and [Gallier, Chapter 5](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~jean/gma-v2-chap5.pdf).
