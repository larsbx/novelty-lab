# N9 — Finite subspace arithmetic

**Status:** candidate

For subspaces of a fixed finite vector space \(V=\mathbb F_q^n\),
use \(U+W\) for span and \(U\cap W\) for shared constraints. Identify a
subspace by a canonical exact basis. Compare the span and intersection
interfaces with row reduction and witness membership. The modular
identity has a containment premise. Keep it separate from unrestricted
distributivity.

Start with \(\mathbb F_3^3\), including the zero and full subspaces,
coincident and nested subspaces, and dependent generating lists. Compare
canonical outputs under basis changes and verify the dimension identity
\(\dim(U+W)+\dim(U\cap W)=\dim U+\dim W\). For the distributivity
control, take three distinct one-dimensional subspaces \(A,B,C\) in
one two-dimensional subspace. Then \(A\cap(B+C)=A\) while
\((A\cap B)+(A\cap C)=0\).

An optional exterior lift encodes a basis as a projective blade.
The wedge of two basis blades represents the direct sum when the
concatenated basis is independent. A vanishing wedge signals dependence
and requires a separate exact span or meet calculation. Compare with the
subspace lattice, Plücker coordinates and the pinned exact Gram and
exterior primitives before selecting a certificate interface. Reusable
field, rank and exterior kernels belong in finite-math-kernels.
