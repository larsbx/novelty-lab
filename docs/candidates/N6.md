## Research object and conventions

The objects are isomorphism classes of finite simple undirected loopless
graphs, including the empty graph. In
$K_0^\square(\mathsf{FinGraph})$, disjoint union is addition, Cartesian
product is multiplication, and $K_1$ is the multiplicative unit. Counts
extend additively to the ring of differences. The vertex-count character is
$\varepsilon(G)=|V(G)|$; coefficients and count targets here are integers
(or characteristic-zero rationals), not counts modulo a prime.

For a connected pattern $\Gamma$ **with at least one edge**, let
$N_\Gamma(G)=\operatorname{inj}(\Gamma,G)/|\operatorname{Aut}\Gamma|$
count ordinary, not necessarily induced, subgraphs. The descriptive term
*fiber-captured* means that every such embedding into $G\square H$ has a
constant coordinate, for all finite $G,H$. With these conventions its
count formulation is

$$
N_\Gamma(G\square H)=N_\Gamma(G)|V(H)|+|V(G)|N_\Gamma(H).
$$

The embedding condition is exactly Cartesian S-primeness (Klavžar–Lipovec–
Petkovšek 2002, §1). The count identity is its elementary algebraic
re-expression, not evidence of a new graph class. The qualification on
$\Gamma$ corrects the provisional dossier: $K_1$ lies in a fiber, but
$N_{K_1}=\varepsilon$ is a character, not an $\varepsilon$-derivation.
Disconnected patterns, induced embeddings, isometric embeddings, graph
homomorphisms and modular counts require separate statements.

### Classification

- **Classical/imported:** Cartesian factorization; the monoid/polynomial ring;
  derivations at any fixed ring character; S-primeness, its existing
  characterizations and recognition complexity; spectral walk convolution;
  differential-operator definitions.
- **Potentially useful synthesis:** translate existing S-prime embedding
  criteria into count derivations and verifiable negative witnesses; present
  invariant-specific calculus with explicit conventions and attribution.
  Usefulness does not establish originality.
- **Theorem candidates awaiting a fresh exact gate:** general ordinary-cycle
  product formulas with injectivity corrections, and their order/symbol
  statements beyond routine spectral consequences. No theorem is
  cleared or declared novel by this audit.
- **Open targets:** precisely specified higher-cycle statements, restricted
  S-prime recognition/certificate questions not already covered, and an
  explicit Cartesian-compatible algebraic bridge. The unrestricted
  characterization, recognition complexity, and generic other-character
  derivation module are removed from the proposed open-question list.

## Terminology and mathematical controls

“Cartesian-prime” is weaker than S-prime. For example, $P_3$ is
Cartesian-prime but embeds across two directions in $K_2\square K_2$.
Connected subgraphs need not equal products of their projections.
*Quasiprime* occurs as historical terminology for S-prime; *quasi Cartesian
product* in local product-recognition papers has a different meaning.
Chastand's *fiber-complemented* concerns gated/prefiber projections and must
not be used as a synonym. His accessible abstract does not settle every
theorem in the full paper; the S-prime sources settle the present collision
positively without relying on that missing access.

Preserve these controls in any downstream proof or experiment:

- $K_1$ is the exceptional count character, not a pattern derivation.
- Mixed embeddings are ordinary injective embeddings; neither a
  homomorphism nor an induced/isometric embedding result transfers silently.
- A negative certificate must exhibit both varying coordinates and an
  injective map; a small-product search cannot certify a universal positive.
- Triangle-free does not mean bipartite: $C_5$ and $C_7$ have nonzero odd
  closed-walk counts.
- Ordinary cycles are injective cycle embeddings modulo automorphisms;
  traces also count backtracking and repeated vertices.
- Principal symbols require a stated normalization; divided symbols change
  factorial coefficients.

## Stop conditions and revised research order

The original stop condition “an equivalent fiber-capture characterization
already exists” is **triggered**, as is the concern about classical ring and
spectral inputs being presented as new. Revise downstream headlines and
open-problem lists before research resumes.

Proceed in this order within the standalone repository:

1. Import the exact manuscript statements, correct the $K_1$ hypothesis,
   and attribute S-prime results and classical algebra.
2. Specify ordinary cycle counts, domain and symbol normalization; compare
   every coefficient with cycle/Hamming/hypercube counting sources before
   any theorem-level claim.
3. Implement existing mixed-embedding/path-coloring certificates with
   independent replay; gate any claimed algorithmic improvement separately.
4. Formulate specific restricted recognition or algebraic-bridge questions.
5. Formalize only statements whose proof and source boundaries are settled;
   consider the Vizing consumer relationship afterwards.

An unsuccessful query, finite computational agreement or a new name is
never a go decision for novelty.
